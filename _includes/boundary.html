{%- comment -%} The boundary. Everything hatchward runs sits inside one rule the reader owns, and beside it is the full list of what crosses that rule. Post-cutover there is exactly one local component (the runner, one per machine) and exactly two things it ever calls out to: the hosted control plane that hands out assignments, and whichever model vendor the container doing the work calls. Both crossings are drawn, deliberately, rather than collapsed into one — they are different traffic, to different places, and a reader deciding what to allow through a firewall needs both named. The control-plane crossing is a session, not a request/response poll: the runner dials wss:///v1/runners/socket and holds it open, receiving assignments pushed down it rather than asking for them on a timer (see internal/hostedrunner/websocket.go and client.go). Say "session"/"receives" here, never "poll". Brand marks are simplified to one ink. This is a wiring diagram, an 18px outline reads where a lockup does not. {%- endcomment -%}
