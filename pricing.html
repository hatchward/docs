Every plan

The same product on every plan. Only the limits change.

  • The Hatchward console: projects, runners, and work as it runs
  • The project setup wizard, from board to publish
  • Notion, Mahfouz and GitHub Projects as ticket sources
  • Repository access through the GitHub App, with no tokens typed in
  • Every assignment in its own container
  • Credentials that stay on your machines and never reach Hatchward
  • One-time enrollment codes for each machine
  • The runner for macOS and Linux, as a boot service

Not in the price

model usage

Agents call your own model vendor accounts, so that spend goes straight to the vendor at your rates. Hatchward adds nothing per task or per token.

Not in the price

the machines

Runners are machines you already own or rent: a build box, a cloud VM, a Mac under a desk. There's no hosted compute to buy and no queue to wait in.

Questions

How the limits and billing work.

What counts as a project, a runner and an assignment?

A project is one published setup in the console: a board, its repositories, and how tickets are routed. A runner is one enrolled machine. An assignment is one ticket being worked in its own container; "at once" is how many can run across your account at the same moment. Tickets beyond that wait until one finishes.

Can I set things up before I pay?

Yes. Creating an account, connecting integrations and drafting a project need no plan. A subscription is needed to publish a project, enroll a runner and start new assignments.

How do I change plans or cancel?

From Plan & billing in the console, which opens Stripe's customer portal, where you manage or cancel the subscription.

What happens if a payment fails?

New work stays available while the payment is retried, and the console tells you it needs attention. If the subscription ends, new work pauses; assignments already running can still finish and report their results.

What if I reach a limit?

The console says which limit you've reached when you try to publish another project or enroll another runner. Move to a larger plan, or remove a runner or project you no longer use.
